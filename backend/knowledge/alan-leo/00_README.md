# AstroGuide Knowledge Pack: Alan Leo, *Esoteric Astrology* (1913)

## Purpose

This package converts Alan Leo's *Esoteric Astrology: A Study in Human
Nature* into a source-aware knowledge layer for AstroGuide.

It is not intended to make Leo's system the universal truth of the
application. It preserves his interpretive method, technical vocabulary,
esoteric framework, and chart-synthesis logic while keeping
source-specific metaphysical claims distinguishable from astronomical
facts and from other schools of astrology.

## Source basis

The first part was checked against the user's scanned copy. The complete
1913 edition was cross-checked against public bibliographic and
digitized sources, including the complete chapter structure and the
second- and third-part contents.

The 1913 edition contains: - Part I: Studies of Influences and Their
Effects, Chapters I-XIV - Part II: Explanatory Diagrams and Illustrative
Horoscopes, Chapters XV-XIX plus an esoteric interpretation - Part III:
Laws of Occult Development and Initiation, Chapters XX-XXV

## Three-layer rule

### Deterministic application logic

Keep in JavaScript: - planetary positions - house cusps and house
membership - aspect geometry and orb - angular distance - planetary
speed and retrogradation when used - dignity calculations - rulership -
sign polarity, element, modality - decanate and other mathematically
defined subdivisions - any star-map geometry that AstroGuide explicitly
chooses to implement

### Knowledge layer

Use these Markdown files for: - interpretive principles -
source-specific correspondences - chart synthesis - Leo's hierarchy of
factors - esoteric doctrine - historical terminology - qualifications
and source provenance

### Prompt layer

Prompts should tell the model how to use the knowledge: - distinguish
fact from interpretation - distinguish Alan Leo's doctrine from general
astrology - synthesize rather than recite - preserve hierarchy - avoid
inventing chart facts - use esoteric doctrine only when relevant or
requested

## Retrieval

Do not inject the whole package into every chat request. Retrieve only
relevant modules.
